How Layover works

Layover is an AI travel planner for people in the UK. Tell it about a trip in your own words, and it compares places and plans your days.

Every figure shows its source and how old it is. Booking is a sandbox demo: no real booking or payment.

1. What Layover does

You describe a trip, for example “somewhere warm in May, about £800 for two”. Layover asks for what is missing and shows your brief for you to confirm. It then compares three places side by side, and plans the days for the place you choose.

The figures come from real travel sources. Each one is labelled Live, Cached with its age, Fallback or Unavailable, so you can judge it yourself.

2. The agent team

Layover uses a team of agents. An agent is a program that uses an AI model for one narrow job. A supervisor passes work between them and keeps the shared trip.

3. The rules Layover keeps

Six rules apply to everything Layover shows. They were agreed on 19 Nov 2026 (decision 0011).

  1. No invented facts

    A price, rule or opening time comes from a named source, or Layover does not show it.

  2. Every outside value has a label

    Live, Cached with its age, Fallback or Unavailable. You can see how fresh a figure is.

  3. The AI does not do the sums

    Totals, currency conversions and comparisons come from tested code, not from the model.

  4. Missing data is never shown as zero

    If Layover does not know something, it says “Not known”. It never shows a zero or a favourable guess.

  5. No scraping

    Data comes from APIs and feeds that Layover is allowed to use, under their terms.

  6. Travel advice links to the FCDO

    Entry and travel advice always links to the FCDO page and shows its update date. Layover does not tell you a place is free of risk: you read the advice and decide.

4. Where the data comes from

Layover uses these sources, under their own terms. The terms were checked on 19 Nov 2026 and are kept in the data sources list.

  • Travelpayouts: Aviasales Data API

    Gives Lowest cached fares and price calendars.

    Terms Free with a token. The fares come from other people’s searches and are kept for 2 to 7 days, so Layover shows them as Cached.

  • Duffel (test mode)

    Gives Search, offers and orders for the booking demo.

    Terms Free sandbox. Prices are fake and the test airline is fictional, so nothing is real.

  • GOV.UK Content API: FCDO travel advice

    Gives Entry requirements and travel advice for each country, with update dates.

    Terms Free, no key. Open Government Licence.

  • Wikivoyage

    Gives Destination guides.

    Terms Free, no key. The text is CC BY-SA 4.0: Layover credits it and links back.

  • Open-Meteo

    Gives Weather forecasts and history.

    Terms Free for non-commercial use, no key. The data is CC BY 4.0, with a credit link next to it.

  • Frankfurter

    Gives European Central Bank exchange rates.

    Terms Free, no key. Daily reference rates.

  • OurAirports

    Gives Airports, countries and regions.

    Terms Public domain download.

  • Open-Meteo Marine

    Gives Sea surface temperature, waves and swell. The page says accuracy at coastal areas is limited.

    Terms Free for non-commercial use, no key. CC BY 4.0: Layover credits the model providers.

  • sunrise-sunset.org

    Gives Sunrise, sunset, twilight and golden hour for any date and place, in local time.

    Terms No sign-up or key. Layover shows a visible link to sunrise-sunset.org where the data is shown.

  • IANA time zone database

    Gives The history of local time for places worldwide, so Layover can work out the time difference from the UK.

    Terms Offline, public domain.

  • OpenFreeMap

    Gives Vector map tiles and styles for the base map.

    Terms No key and no registration. Attribution is required.

  • Wikipedia and Wikidata

    Gives Article summaries, and structured facts such as population and languages.

    Terms No key. Wikipedia text is CC BY-SA, with a credit and a link to the page. Wikidata is CC0.

  • OpenStreetMap, through Overpass

    Gives Points of interest near a place, and hotel names and locations, with no prices.

    Terms No key. ODbL: Layover credits OpenStreetMap and its contributors. Its wiki says commercial use should use a self-hosted or paid server.

  • Unsplash API

    Gives Destination photos.

    Terms Key and app registration. Layover credits Unsplash and the photographer, with a link to their profile.

  • TfL Unified API

    Gives London transport data, for getting to a London airport.

    Terms Free key. Credit “Powered by TfL Open Data” and “Contains OS data © Crown copyright and database rights 2016”.

  • Tavily search API

    Gives Web search results, for travel questions Layover does not otherwise support.

    Terms API key, free plan of 1,000 credits a month. Its terms say queries may be used for AI training and outputs may be inaccurate.

  • Python holidays library

    Gives Public holidays for 250 country codes.

    Terms Offline library, MIT licence.
