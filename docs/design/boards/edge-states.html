Edge states

Six states the design review found: after Stop, a visa question, a budget that cannot be saved, a place with no photo, opening times that depend on the day, and a place that uses pounds. Each says what is known and what is not, and none shows a made-up value.

1. After the traveller pressed Stop

The partial answer stays. One quiet line says it was stopped, and the traveller can continue or ask again.

What to look at

  • The text stops where the traveller pressed Stop. It is not tidied up or finished, and it has no source line, because Layover has not finished the answer.
  • “You stopped this answer.” is a quiet status line in words. Continue is the main action; Ask again is quieter.
  • The canvas is exactly as before. “Nothing from this answer was added to your trip” says so in words.

2. “Do I need a visa for Portugal?”

Layover gives no visa rule of its own. It points to the FCDO entry requirements page and shows when that page was updated.

What to look at

  • The card links to the FCDO entry requirements page, with “Updated 12 Nov 2026” and the Cached, 1 day old label (sample values).
  • The answer says Layover does not give visa rules. It states no rule and says nothing about how the place is to visit.
  • The card is a side answer: Add to trip is offered, and nothing changes on the canvas until the traveller presses it.

3. A budget that cannot be saved

The budget box holds “eight hundred-ish”. The error says what to type, Save waits, and nothing is searched.

What to look at

  • The error is in words with an icon, in the warning colour, and it names an example: “Type the total in pounds, for example 800.” An empty box shows the same line.
  • Save is disabled until the box holds a number. The hint says nothing is searched until the brief is confirmed.
  • The other chips and Confirm brief stay as they were. The old budget is kept until a new valid one is saved.

4. The chosen place, no photo

Porto has no photo, so a drawn scene stands in: the sun and the sea from the welcome illustration, above the place name.

What to look at

  • The drawing uses the isometric style from the illustration guide, in the illustration colour tokens, in light and dark.
  • The name sits on a plain panel below the drawing. There is no photo credit. “No photo of this place yet” is said quietly.
  • No figure is shown: the card says Porto is being researched. The strip and the folded brief are as usual.

5. Opening times, dates not set

Opening times can differ by weekday, and the trip has only a month. The stop says so and offers Add your dates, instead of showing one set of hours.

What to look at

  • Castelo de São Jorge shows “Opening times depend on the day. Add your dates to see them.” It does not show one set of hours.
  • The stop with no known hours still says “Opening times: Not known”. These are two different states.
  • The brief chip still says “May 2027 · 5 nights”: the month is enough to plan, and Layover does not pick dates.

  • Compare this with the state before: the same stop, the same source line, a different hours row.

7. A place that uses pounds

No exchange rate is needed, so the Money card says so in words and shows no rate and no “£1 = £1”.

What to look at

  • There is no figure and no Live or Cached label, because there is no outside value on the card.
  • “Sample place” stands for any place that uses pounds. The board names no real place, because a place’s currency is a fact that needs a source.
  • The card is not hidden: a gap in the canvas would look like missing data.
