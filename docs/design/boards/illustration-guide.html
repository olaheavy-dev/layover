Illustration guide: isometric

The rules that make every Layover drawing look like it came from the same hand. The owner chose "1: Isometric" on 20 Nov 2026, to match the suitcase logo: friendly, playful, classic travel. "Isometric" means a drawing of solid objects from a fixed corner view, where parallel lines stay parallel and size does not shrink with distance.

The welcome scene, the reference drawing

Every rule below can be seen in this one drawing. It shows on the welcome screen (open the screen). Where a rule says "the scene", it means this.

  • One hero (the suitcase character), up to three helpers, one ground, one sky.
  • Blue only: the Harbour blues from the tokens, never another hue.
  • It is drawn once in the page and shown twice, large on the canvas and small above the question on a phone.

1. The grid: true isometric, 30 degrees

  • Two ground axes slope at 30 degrees from the horizontal, one down to the right and one down to the left. The third axis is straight up.
  • On screen, one step along the first axis moves 0.866 across and 0.5 down. One step along the second moves 0.866 back and 0.5 down. Height moves straight up.
  • Vertical edges stay vertical. Parallel edges never meet: no perspective, no vanishing point.
  • Flat marks on a surface (stripes, joints, a ticket) are drawn on the surface and tilted to its angle, so they sit on it.
  • Nothing is rotated freehand. A moving thing such as the plane keeps the same axes: its long axis follows one of the ground axes, so it can only point up-right, up-left, down-right or down-left.
  • The scene is drawn on a 560 by 420 canvas, with the ground slab starting at the point 280, 208 and each side 140 units long.

2. Shape language: chunky and rounded

  • Rounded corners on every solid. A solid is drawn as its flat faces pulled in by the radius and then stroked with their own colour using round joins, so the corners come out soft. Radius: 3 units on small bases, 5 on suitcases, 8 to 10 on the plane body, 6 to 9 on flat cards.
  • Chunky proportions. A suitcase is about as wide as it is tall. Handles are 6 units thick. Wheels are bigger than real life. Nothing is thinner than 3 units, apart from texture lines (stone joints, stripes), which are allowed to vanish on a phone.
  • Few shapes. A drawing is made of boxes, discs, one cone-like canopy and flat cards. Add detail only when it helps tell one object from another.
  • Friendly, with one face. Only the suitcase character has a face, and it is always the same one: two oval eyes and a smile, in the paper tone. Nothing else smiles.

3. Light and colour: three tones for every solid

The light comes from above and a little to the left. So every solid has a top (the lightest), a left face (middle) and a right face (the darkest). This holds in both themes. The colours are tokens in docs/design/tokens.css, with a light and a dark value each, so a theme change recolours every drawing.

  • Flat things use one tone: --illus-sun for the sun and stickers, --illus-water for the sea, --illus-wave for wave marks, --illus-cut for eyes, windows and notches (the page showing through).
  • --illus-shadow is a soft shadow on the ground, under suitcases.
  • Never a colour written by hand. make check fails on one, in a drawing as anywhere else.
  • In the dark theme the families keep the same order (top lightest) but sit on the night background, so the ink family turns light and the sea turns deep.

4. Line: almost none

  • No outlines. Shapes are told apart by tone, not by a drawn edge. Never a black or text-colour line round a shape.
  • Lines exist only as things: the umbrella pole, handles, straps, the dotted trail, the wave marks and the umbrella's edge. They are 2.4 to 6 units thick, with round ends, and take a colour from the same family as the thing they belong to.
  • The dotted trail has a dash of 1 unit and a gap of 9 with round ends, so it reads as dots.

5. The cast

Every drawing is made from these pieces, so a new scene feels like part of the same world. A new piece needs a reason, and joins this list in the same change.

  • Suitcase character

    The hero. The only piece with a face: two eyes and a smile on the left face, a sun sticker on the right face, a handle, two straps and two wheels. Ink family. One in each scene.

  • Small suitcase

    A cousin with no face, no sticker and plain straps. Sun family (the lighter blue). It makes the hero look bigger and never competes with it.

  • Ticket

    A rounded card lying flat on the ground, with two bars, a notch each side of a dotted fold and a round stamp. No words, no numbers. It is dropped on a phone.

  • Umbrella

    Four stripes, alternating sun and paper tones, an edge line in the dark sun tone, and a round tip. It stands on a small ink base.

  • Plane

    Long body, flat swept wings and a tail, all on the 30 degree axes, heading up and to the right. A dotted trail leaves its tail. Ink body, sun wings.

  • Sun

    A flat disc with twelve short rays, alternately long and short. It is the one piece with no shading and no face: it only ever says 'warm'.

  • Cloud

    Four rounded shapes on a flat base, in the paper tone. Flat, with no shading. Two sizes in the welcome scene.

  • Sea and quay

    A quay (a stone platform) is a thick slab with joints on its sides, standing in a diamond of water with small wave marks. It is the ground every scene stands on.

6. Scale and what survives on a phone

  • Each scene has one hero. The hero is the largest solid and sits near the middle of the ground.
  • The welcome scene is about 490 units wide. On the canvas it shows at about 460 px, so one unit is nearly one pixel. Above the question on a phone it shows 216 px wide, so one unit is about 0.44 px.
  • Details under 6 units across vanish on a phone: the ticket's bars, the stone joints and the wave marks. That is fine for texture, and not fine for anything that has meaning.
  • Each scene has a small form. Drop what no longer reads. In the welcome scene the ticket is dropped on the phone, through the custom property --illus-ticket-display. The hero, the umbrella, the plane and the sun stay.
  • Empty states and error states use the same cast at a smaller scale: the hero on a small piece of ground, with at most one helper.

7. Accessibility and honesty

  • Decorative. A drawing is marked aria-hidden="true" and focusable="false". A screen reader skips it.
  • It never carries information the text does not give. A plane does not mean "flights found". A ticket does not mean "booked". The sun does not mean a forecast. If it matters, the words beside the drawing say it.
  • No words, numbers, signs or logos inside a drawing. No real place and no recognisable landmark: a drawing must not suggest a place Layover has not named (decision 0011, rule 1).
  • No animation for now. If a drawing moves later, it stops with the prefers-reduced-motion setting.
  • Both themes are checked. Look at every new drawing in light and dark, and at 216 px wide, before it goes in. Illustrations are decoration, so the text contrast target does not apply to them, but they are never the only sign of a state.
  • Registered. Each drawing is listed under "Inline illustrations" in docs/assets/README.md before it is committed.

How to add a drawing

  • Sketch it on the 30 degree axes, from the cast.
  • Build solids with rounded faces and the three tones.
  • Take colours from the --illus-* tokens only.
  • Add aria-hidden="true", and write the text that carries the meaning.
  • Check it in light and dark, large and at 216 px.
  • Register it, then run make check.

8. Two more checks

  • One angle. The drawing on the right below is a front view with a slanted top: it looks 3D but follows no grid, so it clashes with everything else. Every piece sits on the same 30 degree axes.
  • Ground. Solids stand on a ground tile, with a soft shadow under the feet. Nothing floats, except the plane and the clouds, which are in the sky.
